# 9. PRIORITY 01 — PROTECT TAXPAYERS

## Title

> **Protect Taxpayers**

## Supporting Line

> **Make every municipal dollar work harder.**

## Approved Copy

> Families, homeowners, renters, seniors and local businesses are all feeling financial pressure. Municipal government must recognize that taxpayers do not have unlimited capacity to absorb higher costs.
>
> As Councillor, I will support disciplined budgeting, clearer justification for major spending and greater transparency about the long-term financial impact of municipal decisions.

## Action Points

- Scrutinize major spending proposals.
- Ask for lifecycle and long-term operating costs before supporting major capital projects.
- Prioritize essential services and infrastructure.
- Support clearer public reporting on municipal spending.
- Look for efficiencies before automatically turning to taxpayers.
- Consider seniors, families and residents on fixed incomes when major financial decisions are made.

## Guardrails

Do **not** promise:

- arbitrary tax freezes;
- guaranteed tax reductions;
- that one councillor can unilaterally lower taxes;
- that every tax increase is avoidable.

The focus should be:

- fiscal discipline;
- value for money;
- transparency;
- spending priorities.

# 10. PRIORITY 02 — FIX FLOODING & AGING INFRASTRUCTURE

## Title

> **Fix Flooding & Aging Infrastructure**

## Supporting Line

> **Put infrastructure, engineering evidence and public safety first.**

## Approved Copy

> Recent flooding has reinforced something many residents already know: infrastructure cannot be treated as an afterthought.
>
> Roads, stormwater systems, sanitary sewers, watermains and drainage infrastructure are some of the most important assets a municipality manages.
>
> As Councillor, I will advocate for infrastructure decisions based on engineering evidence, asset condition, public safety and long-term risk — not simply what is easiest to postpone.

## Action Points

- Advocate for transparent infrastructure condition assessments.
- Identify high-risk sewer, stormwater, drainage, watermain and road infrastructure.
- Push for clear prioritization based on engineering evidence and risk.
- Support public reporting on planned infrastructure projects and completed work.
- Examine basement flooding mitigation and stormwater capacity.
- Support coordinated planning between new development and available infrastructure capacity.
- Ask whether sufficient sewer, water and stormwater capacity exists when reviewing major development.
- Advocate for long-term capital planning that does not simply transfer infrastructure problems to future generations.
- Seek federal, provincial and regional funding opportunities where appropriate.
- Improve communication with residents before, during and after major infrastructure work.

## Do Not Claim

Do not publish claims that:

- flooding was deliberately caused by municipal politicians;
- specific politicians knowingly caused sewer failures;
- a specific “60-year infrastructure plan” exists unless verified documentation is supplied;
- engineering advice was intentionally ignored unless verified municipal documentation is supplied;
- the City is automatically legally liable for all flooding;
- flooding was entirely “man-made.”

Keep this section:

- serious;
- engineering-focused;
- evidence-based;
- practical.

# 21. BEFORE WE SPEND, ASK THE RIGHT QUESTIONS

## Heading

> **Before We Spend, Ask the Right Questions**

For major capital projects, show the following decision framework:

1. Is it necessary?
2. What problem does it solve?
3. What is the full construction cost?
4. What will it cost annually to operate?
5. What alternatives were considered?
6. Can existing facilities be upgraded or reused?
7. Are grants or partnerships available?
8. What infrastructure projects are competing for the same resources?
9. What is the long-term impact on taxpayers?
10. How will success be measured?
